# Capital Structure

Establishes what the buyer would actually have to fund at completion: every
facility, every obligation that behaves like debt, and the cash that can be
counted against it.

Do not recommend invest or pass. Do not calculate leverage from an incomplete
facility schedule — a partial schedule gives a wrong ratio, not an approximate
one. Only freely available cash counts against debt in full. Source every
net-debt line.

Sector focus (if known): [SECTOR]
Geography focus (if known): [GEOGRAPHY]

1. Schedule every facility from the loan documents and the ledger: lender,
drawn balance, rate, repayment profile, maturity, security, guarantees,
covenants and headroom. Reconcile to the balance sheet.

2. List items that behave like debt even though they sit elsewhere: overdue
trade creditors beyond normal terms, declared and unpaid dividends, deferred
or contingent consideration, capital expenditure creditors, lease obligations,
unfunded employee obligations, tax arrears.

3. Split cash into freely available, restricted, and the minimum the business
needs to operate. Only the first counts against debt in full.

4. Identify change-of-control clauses, prepayment penalties and consents the
transaction would trigger.

5. Present net debt as: gross debt, less available cash, plus debt-like items,
with every line sourced.
