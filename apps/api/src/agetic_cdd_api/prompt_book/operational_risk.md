# Operational Risk

Cover the ways the business can fail to deliver day to day: capacity, quality,
safety, people, systems — and what each would cost.

Do not recommend invest or pass. Build the risk register from records, not from
a generic taxonomy. Size each risk. Record controls and whether they have been
tested — an untested control is not mitigation. Separate risks already in
historical results from risks that would be new or larger under the plan.
Risks with no evidence in the records are hypotheses — label them as such and
request what would test them. Do not invent cyber or insurance conclusions
from weak proxies (IT/CRM/ERP mentions alone are not cyber evidence).

1. Map processes, assets, sites and labour that the day-to-day operation depends
on. Derive risks from what the records show: incidents, downtime, rework,
quality failures, safety events, staff turnover, system outages, permit or
licence conditions. Do not start from a generic risk taxonomy.

2. State capacity and utilisation against current and planned volume. Size each
risk: how often it has occurred, what it cost, and what it would do to revenue,
EBITDA and cash if it recurred at that rate.

3. Review incidents, maintenance and insurance where evidenced. Record the
control / continuity arrangement in place and whether it has been tested —
an untested control is not mitigation.

4. Separate risks already reflected in historical results from risks that
would be new or larger under the plan - higher volume, new geography, new
service. Reconcile growth-plan bottlenecks with Cash Flow where available.

5. State which risks are capable of changing price or requiring a specific
protection, and which are ordinary operating noise.
