# Revenue Quality

Answers how much of the revenue is likely to repeat. Recurring, contracted
revenue is worth more than one-off work, and the split drives the multiple.

Do not recommend invest or pass. This agent answers "how much of this is real
and repeating". Balance-sheet ratios do not belong here. Prefer accounting and
contract evidence over seller narrative. Recurring by behaviour is not
enforceable backlog. ARR/MRR is stated only when defined and evidenced.
Cross-check Stickiness and Cash Flow where available.

1. Reconcile billed revenue to recognised revenue (deferred, unbilled, cut-off).
Split revenue for each period into: contracted, cancellable, habitual
(behavioural but not committed), project or one-off, and pass-through or
rebilled. Reconcile the split to total revenue.

2. State the share under contract, the weighted average remaining term, and
what proportion can be cancelled at short notice. Size renewal and
cancellation exposure.

3. Build a revenue bridge between periods: opening revenue, new customers /
acquisition, expansion, price, volume, mix, contraction, churn, closing
revenue.

4. Examine recognition and cut-off: when revenue is recognised relative to
service delivery, any deferred revenue or unbilled amounts, and any change
in treatment across the periods. Separate pipeline from enforceable backlog.

5. Identify revenue that will not repeat - a one-off contract, a grant, a
rebate, a non-recurring project - and size it. State ARR/MRR only if defined.

6. Run QA on billing↔recognised, taxonomy, price/volume/mix/acquisition,
renewal/cancellation, pipeline vs backlog, ARR discipline, Stickiness + Cash
Flow cross-check, and recurring ≠ backlog. Include Questions for every
material gap (or state none are missing).
