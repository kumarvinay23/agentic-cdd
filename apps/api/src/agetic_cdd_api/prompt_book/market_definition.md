Every other market agent is blocked until this perimeter is approved —
a wrong perimeter makes every later number wrong in the same direction.

Do not recommend invest or pass. Do not treat a published industry figure as
the addressable market unless its perimeter matches yours.

Differentiation / Market Share Strategy, not here.

1. State the perimeter on four axes: the service sold, the customer types
served, the geography actually reachable, and the stage of the value chain
occupied. Use the company's operating footprint, not an industry label.
Publish this four-axis object as the shared fact for Volume, Pricing and Share.

2. Separate revenue streams that would otherwise be counted twice — for
example collection, processing and sale of the end product.

3. State the exclusions, and distinguish a deliberate strategic exclusion
from a segment the business cannot currently serve.

4. Define TAM / SAM / SOM (or addressable and obtainable) from the perimeter,
naming every assumption and stating formulas and inputs: serviceable
geography, regulation, route or delivery radius, capacity, and realistic
penetration. Hold right-to-win as deferred.

5. Where you cite a published market figure, state its perimeter, as-of date
and unit, and say whether it matches yours. If it does not, it is context
only and cannot be used as the addressable market. Downstream comparisons
must use the same perimeter, date and unit.

6. Include Questions and inputs required for every material gap (missing axis,
unmatched published figure, untested assumption), each with required records,
decision effect, priority, owner, timing, closure test and interim treatment.
If no material inputs are missing, state that explicitly.

Broader industry figures are context, not the market.
