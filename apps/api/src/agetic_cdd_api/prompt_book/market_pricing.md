1. Calculate realised net price by service, segment and period from invoices
or the billing ledger, after discounts, credits and surcharges. Normalise
scope, discounts and taxes so comparisons are like-for-like. Keep list
price, realised price and mix effects separate.

2. Compare with named competitors on an equivalent service specification,
using dated quotes or published prices. Unnamed comparators are not
acceptable.

3. Build a price-volume-mix bridge from one period to the next, and extend
to cost so the bridge reconciles to reported revenue, gross profit and
EBITDA where evidenced.

4. Examine what happened after past price increases: churn, downgrades and
volume in the following periods, with the number of customers affected.
Describe this as observed response, not as elasticity, unless the data
support a measured relationship. Do not invent elasticity.

5. State whether contracts carry escalators, and what proportion of revenue
they cover.

6. Cross-check material pricing claims against Growth Opportunities, Revenue
Quality and Sensitivity — flag conflicts as unresolved, naming both sources.

7. Include Questions and inputs required for every material gap (missing
invoice ASP, unnamed comps only, unreconciled PVM, untested response),

closure test and interim treatment. If no material inputs are missing,
state that explicitly.
