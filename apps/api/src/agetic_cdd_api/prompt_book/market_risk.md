# Market Risk

Covers the external things that could damage the case: competitor moves,
customer losses, regulatory change, demand cycles — sized against this
company's own numbers.

Do not recommend invest or pass. Argue likelihood from evidence, not
arbitrary scores. Quantify downside where exposure is bounded. State
early-warning indicators. Separate risks that change price from those
that change structure. Risks that cannot be sized are still reported,
with what would size them. Reject unnamed or generic threats. Do not
duplicate Internal Risk (key-person, controls, cash/covenant strain,
governance) — hand those off or cross-check.

1. Identify the external risks that matter for this business and geography.
For each row state: trigger, population exposed, timing, mechanism, and
mitigation (or what would mitigate). Size the revenue or margin exposed
in dollars where possible.

2. Argue likelihood from evidence - competitor behaviour, contract renewal
dates, policy timetables, historical cycles. Use evidenced ranges only —
do not assign numeric probabilities without a basis.

3. Quantify the downside where the exposure is bounded: what happens to
EBITDA if the largest contract is lost, or if volume falls to the level
seen in the last downturn.

4. State the early-warning indicator for each material risk - the thing the
buyer should watch monthly after completion.

5. Separate risks that change price from those that change structure - an
escrow, an earn-out, a specific indemnity or a condition precedent.

6. Keep Market Risk external. Cross-check Internal Risk where available so
key-person, control, cash/covenant and governance items are not restated
here. Reject unnamed threats (e.g. "market risk", "macro headwinds") that
lack a named trigger and population.

likelihood, early warnings, price vs structure, Internal Risk non-overlap,
and unnamed-threat rejection. Include Questions for every material gap
(or state none are missing).
