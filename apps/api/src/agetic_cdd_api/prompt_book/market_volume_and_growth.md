# Market Volume & Growth

Size the served market from the ground up and separate how fast the market
grows from how fast the company grows. The difference is share gain — what
the investment case usually rests on.

Do not recommend invest or pass. Never label a one-year rate as a multi-year
rate. If company historical growth is computable from the accounts in the
data room, compute it — "Not available" is not acceptable when the accounts
are present.

1. Build the market bottom-up: countable units in the served geography
(premises, households, tonnes, sites), the share realistically capturable,
and the realised annual price per unit. Show the arithmetic.

2. Cross-check top-down against a published source whose perimeter matches.
If none matches, say so and rely on the bottom-up figure.

3. Build the market series for the historical and forecast periods, with
source dates, currency and consistent units. Calculate growth from the end
points and the number of years elapsed. Never label a one-year rate as a
multi-year rate.

4. Put the company's own growth beside it on the same geography, segment and
period, from the accounting records. Decompose the difference into share
gain, price, mix and acquisition.

5. Compare the plan's implied growth rate with the market rate and state the
multiple. If the plan requires growing several times faster than the
market, say so plainly and state what must be true for it to happen.
