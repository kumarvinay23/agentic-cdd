About this guide 
This guide explains how DiligenceIQ turns an uploaded data room into a checked, traceable databook — 
and where your judgement enters that process. 
THE POINT  
Use the databook only when the 
evidence supports it. 
YOUR ROLE  
Resolve exceptions; do not 
simply clear warnings. 
DONE MEANS  
Material rows tie, and 
judgement calls are recorded. 
 
Working principle: DiligenceIQ is conservative by design. If a row cannot be proven, it stays visible 
but out of the databook until a reviewer decides what the evidence supports. 
1.  The workflow at a glance 
A useful databook is not just a table of extracted numbers. It is a chain of evidence: source document, 
extracted row, mapped metric, arithmetic check, reviewer decision, and final databook output. 
DiligenceIQ builds the first pass. It reads the data room, extracts tables, maps line items, and checks 
whether statement blocks add back to the source's own printed subtotals. The reviewer handles anything 
the app cannot prove on its own. 
 
Figure 1. The trust loop: DiligenceIQ reads and checks; reviewers resolve exceptions and rerun when needed. 
Reviewer rule: Your job is not to make warnings disappear. Your job is to leave the databook in a 
state where another deal team member can understand why every material number was accepted,

2.  Why the system refuses numbers 
Diligence files are rarely tidy. They arrive as scans, PDFs, spreadsheets, board packs, management models, 
and exported schedules. OCR can lose commas, read a percentage as a value, mistake a segment row for a 
total, or treat a quarter as a year. 
The app therefore starts from zero trust. A statement block is usable only when its rows reconcile to the 
subtotal printed in the source. If the block does not tie, the affected rows are held out. They remain 
visible for review, but they do not silently enter the databook. 
Simple example: If the source says Revenue 100 plus Other income 20 equals Total income 120, 
DiligenceIQ checks that arithmetic itself. If the printed total and component rows do not reconcile, 
the app cannot guess which value is wrong, so it holds the block back. 
One check is one block for one period. Seven statement blocks across three years can therefore produce 
21 checks. This is why a single source file can generate a large number of held-out rows.

Sources disagree 
A source disagreement is not always an extraction problem. It may reveal a genuine conflict: draft versus 
final version, management view versus audited filing, group versus segment scope, or quarterly data 
being compared with annual data. 
 
Figure 4. Full screen: the expanded disagreement shows candidate values, captions, source documents, and the selected 
value. 
WHAT TO NOTICE  
The highlighted block shows the competing values 
and why one candidate was selected. 
YOUR MOVE 
Compare the captions and source documents. 
Keep the governing value, correct or drop the bad 
read, or record the contradiction as a diligence 
issue. 
 
• Check whether the sources are covering the same entity, period, and metric definition. 
• Prefer the source that governs the deal workstream, not simply the largest or latest number . 
• Document true contradictions; do not bury them by forcing a silent override. 
Unread 
Unread commonly appears as Missing period. It does not automatically mean the app failed. Intro pages, 
disclaimers, entity ledgers, and tables of contents often should stay out of the databook.

Not landed 
Not landed means the app found something row-shaped, but it could not land the row in the analysis 
model. Common causes are an implausible fiscal year, a ratio read as an amount, or a page number read 
as data.

YOUR MOVE

the source structure or rules and rerun rather 
than forcing a weak row through.

Make the correction match the fault. Correcting, dropping, vouching, and re-reading mean different 
things, and the difference matters later when another reviewer asks why the databook changed.

7.  Use Findings as the trust ledger 
04   Use Findings when the question is source-level. 
Derived data shows rows. Findings shows documents. When a warning says many rows were held out, 
Findings helps you find the file that is causing the trust problem. 
A clean-looking document list is not automatically a pass. A file can have no financial table to check, which 
means it neither passed nor failed. Open the source row when the contribution is unclear .

9.  When the source document must be fixed 
Some problems do not belong inside the app. If a scan is unreadable, figures only exist inside a picture, or 
a spreadsheet is not laid out like a table, fix the source evidence instead of hand-correcting a long list of 
rows. 
• Ask for a cleaner copy, ideally a native PDF or original Excel file instead of a scan. 
• Re-upload a corrected version after fixing source formatting. 
• Build a clean supporting schedule when the source cannot be made readable. 
A clean schedule should have: 
• One header row, with years across the top and line items down the side. 
• One number per cell, with no text mixed into number cells. 
• No merged cells, blank spacer rows, or footnote markers inside number cells. 
• Currency and units stated clearly, such as GBP millions or USD thousands. 
• Subtotals that actually add to the rows above them. 
10.  Current limitations 
• There is no dedicated review screen yet. Editing happens inline in All line items. 
• Users can only edit rows that are headed for the databook. Other material may still need source-
document review. 
• Users cannot overwrite a disagreement directly on the disagreement panel. That is deliberate: the 
conflict must be resolved through source review, row correction, or report commentary.
